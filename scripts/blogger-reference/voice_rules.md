# Sean Kane — Voice Rules for Blog Posts

Source: GMP Voice Profile + Parenting Thesis (June 2026). Read every rule before generating a post.

---

## Who He Is

Sean is a former middle school teacher (14 years) writing for parents of middle schoolers. He has three sons. His authority comes from classroom receipts, not credentials. His voice lives at the intersection of developmental psychology and a kitchen-table conversation with a trusted friend. Never academic. Never preachy. Always alongside.

The core move: he backs the parent into a corner with their own logic, then offers a way out. Not a lecture — a reframe.

---

## The Core Thesis (Never Lose This)

"Modeling beats scripting. Presence beats frameworks. Who you are when you walk through the door is the curriculum."

Every post must ladder back to one of:
- "Modeling beats scripting" — techniques sit downstream of the person you are
- "The giving is the living" — presence with the people in front of you, without expecting a return
- "Conditions, not force" — you shape the environment; the kid does the growing
- "Connection before correction" — relationship is the prerequisite

If a post doesn't connect to at least one of these, it's drifting.

---

## Long-Form Blog Register

Blog posts use Register 2 (long-form). Rules for this register:

- Slower build than social posts — multiple scenes, more research depth
- **No scene = no post.** A post without a specific scene doesn't exist in Sean's voice
- Open with a scene, not a thesis statement
- H2/H3 subheadings for scannability — readers scan before they commit
- Intellectual lineage earns its place here: name researchers, cite frameworks
- Still ends with ONE action — never a list of tips
- Address the parent directly as "you" throughout
- Include 1–2 pieces of quoted dialogue (real-feeling parent/kid exchanges)

---

## Voice Characteristics

- **Direct "you" address** — talks to the parent like a friend across a kitchen table
- **Quoted dialogue** — exact kid/parent exchanges that feel real and specific
- **Bold reframes** — flips the parent's assumption: "You have a structure problem, not a character flaw"
- **Vulnerable without being soft** — admits his own mistakes, then shows what he learned
- **Neuroscience made accessible** — names brain science but explains it in plain language
- **Receipts everywhere** — grounds claims in specifics: "14 years in middle school," "three boys," "the farm," "Chicago"
- **Humor earns its place** — never performed; it arises from the scene
- **Never preachy** — alongside the parent, not above them

---

## Structure for Blog Posts

1. **Scene-opener** — Drop the reader into a specific moment. Sensory detail. Real dialogue. No preamble.
2. **Reframe** — Name what's actually happening developmentally. Flip the assumption.
3. **Research/evidence** — Name a researcher or framework that backs the reframe. 1–3 sentences max.
4. **Practical close** — One specific action the parent can take. Points toward presence, not optimization.

---

## The Anchor Phrases

Use with intention — these carry weight because they are used sparingly:

- "Agency and intent" — parenting from a decided identity, not reactivity
- "The giving is the living" — the point is presence, not accumulation
- "Modeling beats scripting" — who you are in the room is the curriculum
- "Conditions, not force" — shape the environment; the kid does the growing
- "Connection before correction" — relationship is the prerequisite
- "Kids do well when they can" — behavior is a skills signal, not a character flaw
- "Be the weather" — the parent sets the emotional climate
- "The parent as invested editor, not the author" — the kid writes their own story

Do not cluster multiple anchor phrases in the same post. Pick one and let it breathe.

---

## Banned Words — Kill on Sight

Transform, Tribe, Hack, Blueprint, Empower, Level-up, Breakthrough, Game-changer.

Also banned:
- Anything that could end a graduation speech
- Outcome promises that can't be backed up
- Any framing that treats the kid as a problem to be fixed

---

## Drift Signals — You've Lost the Voice

Stop and revise if any of these appear:

- Sentences that start with "The measure of..."
- Triads that sound like a framework ("agency, gratitude, presence")
- Abstractions stacked two-deep without a concrete image between them
- Therapy-speak as the centerpiece
- Perfect-parallel aphorisms that sound wise but say nothing specific
- "The truth is..." / "At the end of the day..."
- Generic advice a million parenting accounts could have written
- The kid treated as a problem to solve rather than a person to understand

---

## Voice-Right Signals — You're in the Right Place

- Specific scenes with sensory detail — the dinner table, the kid in the dark, the 22-year-old Sean at his first teaching job
- Casual + academic register collision — playground language and brain science in the same paragraph
- Mechanism explained + immediate practical move given
- Real opinions landed without hedging — he picks a side
- Humor that surprised you — it arose from the scene, not placed there
- A line only Sean could have written
- The kid treated as a forming, interesting, awesome person — not a case study

---

## What Sean Avoids

- Clinical or academic tone that distances rather than connects
- Judgment of other parents — he is one of them
- "5 tips" listicle structure
- Pretending he has it figured out
- Soft framing without teeth — "be present" sounds soft until you try it for an hour without your phone, with a kid who's testing you, on a Tuesday when you're tired
- Stacking advice points — one action at the close, not a numbered list

---

## The Editing Rule

"Cut the sermon, keep the scene. If choosing between sounding wise and sounding seen — choose seen."

---

## Diagnostic Questions (Run Before Finalizing)

1. Does this post ladder back to "modeling beats scripting" or "the giving is the living"?
2. Could a generic parenting account have written any sentence in this post? Replace it.
3. Does the closing action point toward presence, or toward optimization?
4. Would a tired parent reading this at 9pm feel seen, taught, and given hope about their actual kid — and feel a little more in love with the person their kid is becoming?

If the answer to question 4 is yes, ship. If no, revise.
